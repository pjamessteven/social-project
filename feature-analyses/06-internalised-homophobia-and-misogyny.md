# Internalised homophobia and internalised misogyny/misandry

*Feature tags: internalised homophobia 379; internalised misogyny/misandry 1,327.*

## Headline

These two features capture the sex- and sexuality-based drivers of transition
that dominate much of the community's own retrospective analysis.

- **Internalised homophobia is strongly tied to actual same-sex attraction.**
  Among accounts whose sexuality *before* transition reads as homosexual,
  **26.6%** score internalised homophobia ≥ 0.8 — versus 13.3% of bisexual,
  3.9% of heterosexual and 1.5% of unclear accounts. After detransition the
  pattern holds (24.1% of now-homosexual accounts).
- **Internalised misogyny/misandry skews heavily female**: 12.2% of women name
  it as the primary transition reason versus 4.5% of men, and it remains a top-5
  driver overall (504 as primary reason; 1,327 tagged).

## Sexual orientation shifts

Jev's *sexuality before vs after* choice shows movement after detransition:
homosexual 881 → 921, heterosexual 411 → 654, bisexual 330 → 475, "unclear"
3,331 → 2,875. A meaningful share of accounts clarify or shift their orientation
once transition pressure is off.

## Why it's interesting

These features operationalise the two most-cited psychodynamic explanations in
detrans writing — and the data supports both being real but **subgroup-specific**,
not universal: internalised homophobia is a same-sex-attraction phenomenon, while
internalised misogyny/misandry is predominantly a female phenomenon. They are
distinct axes and should not be lumped together.
