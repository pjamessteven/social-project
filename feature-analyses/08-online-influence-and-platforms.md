# Online influence and platforms

*Influenced online 448 (8.9%) · peer influence 221 (4.4%) · ROGD 96 (1.9%).*

## Headline

Online influence is real but **not the dominant story**: fewer than 1 in 10
accounts is tagged as having transition driven by online communities. Peer
influence (friends/partners) is smaller still.

## Platforms named as the primary influence

| Platform | Users | Share |
|---|---|---|
| None | 2,204 | 43.8% |
| Reddit | 1,450 | 28.8% |
| Other | 663 | 13.2% |
| YouTube | 308 | 6.1% |
| Tumblr | 179 | 3.6% |
| Discord | 82 | 1.6% |
| Twitter | 73 | 1.5% |
| TikTok | 71 | 1.4% |

Reddit dominates — unsurprising for a cohort drawn from a Reddit community —
while the TikTok narrative, so prominent in media coverage, covers just 1.4% of
accounts.

## Age pattern

Among accounts with a usable transition age (10–40, n = 1,288):

- **Under 18**: online influence 19.6% vs **18+**: 14.6%.
- ROGD scored 1.8% (under 18) vs 3.7% (18+); peer influence 7.7% vs 8.3%.

Online influence is meaningfully higher for people who transitioned as minors,
but it is a minority driver in both groups.

## Caveats

- "ROGD" (rapid onset gender dysphoria) is contested terminology and is used
  here only as a model label for sudden-onset adolescent dysphoria.
- Platform answers reflect what accounts *say* shaped them, and age data in the
  timelines contains parsing noise (outliers excluded above).
