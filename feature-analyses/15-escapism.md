# Escapism

*Feature tag "escapism": 1,769 accounts (35.2%).*

## Headline

Over a third of accounts describe transition (at least partly) as an **escape** —
from trauma, depression, social pressures, or a body/self they wanted to leave
behind. It is the largest single *motive-level* feature that isn't also a named
"reason", and it cuts across other categories (trauma, mental health, online
influence).

## Why it's interesting

Escapism is a **retrospective** judgement: people who still endorse transition
rarely frame it as escape (7–13%), while those who detransitioned often do
(42%). This makes it a useful marker of narrative distance from the transition —
and it is conceptually distinct from regret: an account can describe transition
as an escape without necessarily regretting it, and vice versa.

## Caveats

- "Escape" is the model's interpretation of motive language; the underlying
  accounts rarely use the word itself.
- It overlaps with trauma history (36.9%) and mental-health issues (55.5%), but
  is not reducible to either — it names the *function* transition served, not
  the condition behind it.
