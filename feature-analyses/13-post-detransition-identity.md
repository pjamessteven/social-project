# Post-detransition identity

*Where people land after stopping transition.*

## Headline figures

| Feature | Users | Share |
|---|---|---|
| No detransition occurred | 804 | 16.0% |
| Never transitioned | 704 | 14.0% |
| Only transitioned socially | 442 | 8.8% |
| Still identifies as trans | 181 | 3.6% |
| Retransition | 124 | 2.5% |
| Non-binary after detransition | 108 | 2.1% |

## Patterns

- **Non-binary identification after detransition is small (108)** and
  concentrates among retransitioners (7.3%) and detransitioned users (2.8%);
  desisters 1.3%. It is a waypoint for some, not a dominant endpoint.
- **Only-social transition is essentially a desister marker (27.9% of
  desisters vs a single detransitioned account)** — as expected now that
  "desisted" means no medical steps.
- **Retransition is rare (124 tagged; 55 current status)**, and 40 of the 55
  status-retransitioners carry the retransition tag themselves — the model
  separates "went back" from "currently trans" reasonably cleanly.
- **Detransition support networks** are mentioned by 1,103 accounts (21.9%).

## Why it's interesting

The "non-binary after detransition" question was added specifically to test a
common claim; the data shows it is a real but minor pathway (~1 in 50),
concentrated among people who haven't fully left transition behind. Meanwhile
704 accounts never transitioned and 442 only socially — together a large share
of the community's internal diversity.
