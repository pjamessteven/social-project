# Medical footprint

*What accounts disclose about medical transition. Numbers are disclosures, not
medical records.*

## Prevalence

| Intervention | Users | Share | Female | Male |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Took hormones | 2,556 | 50.8% | 1,757 (48.7% of f) | 799 (56.2% of m) |
| Top surgery | 690 | 13.7% | 655 | 35 |
| Bottom surgery | 157 | 3.1% | 38 | 119 |
| Facial surgery | 65 | 1.3% | 4 | 61 |
| Puberty blockers | 148 | 2.9% | 90 | 58 |
| DIY hormones | 89 | 1.8% | 32 | 57 |

## Consequences

- **Irreversible changes**: 1,191 (23.7%) — 26.1% of females, 17.5% of males.
- **Medical complications**: 1,184 (23.5%).
- **Ongoing medical needs**: 938 (18.7%); **currently on hormones**: 369 —
  notably more common among males (14.0%) than females (4.7%).
- **Healthcare system dissatisfaction**: 1,116 (22.2%).

## Sex asymmetry

The surgical profile inverts cleanly: females overwhelmingly had **top surgery**
(18.2% of all females), males had **bottom and facial surgery** (8.4% and 4.3%).
Hormone use is close to half of both groups, and the staying-on-hormones
difference may reflect options and detransition paths rather than regret alone.

## Hormones by current status

Took hormones: retransitioned 90.9%, detransitioned 81.1%, still
trans/questioning 78.1%, questioning 20.4% — and only **1 of 1,497 desisters**
(by definition; that single flag is a known false positive).
